Transform the supplied photograph into a vertical 3:5 tactile paper collage poster. Preserve five core principles: truthful photo anchor, large abstract illustration field, one hue as chromatic structure, visible hand-torn paper edge, and breathable paper surface.

**Canvas & Surface:**
Vertical 3:5 poster on cream/warm ivory paper (similar to #F5F0E6). Flat scan aesthetic with visible paper fiber texture, grain, and ink behavior. No mockup framing, no digital frame, no shadow box.

**Photo–Illustration Allocation:**
- Photography occupies approximately 30–50% of the poster as a truthful anchor. The photographic section preserves the original scene faithfully: subjects, spatial relationships, horizon, gaze direction, path, silhouette, and overlap must remain recognizable. Do not repaint, filter, or stylize the photo section.
- Abstract illustration field occupies approximately 45–70% of the poster. The illustration reinterprets selected source elements rather than tracing them. Use medium abstraction: preserve the core subject and dominant gesture, remove 60–80% of small detail, merge repeated forms (trees, crowds, windows, waves) into larger masses.

**Illustration Field — Choose One Primary Grammar:**
Analyze the photo and select one primary illustration grammar:
- Silhouette-led: one broad dark or gray mass for figures, trees, roofs, boats
- Contour-led: a few broken lines for architecture, paths, coastlines
- Field-led: one irregular ink or halftone field for water, fog, sky, shadow
- Rhythm-led: repeated marks for posts, windows, waves, branches
- Cut-paper-led: simplified organic cutouts for strong shape hierarchy
Use only one primary grammar plus at most one supporting grammar. Active illustrated ink occupies roughly 15–35% of the poster; keep 55–75% of the illustration field quiet (blank paper).

**Compress Dense Detail:**
For foliage-dominant or intricate scenes: retain one main canopy/tree mass, 1–3 directional branch gestures, no more than two secondary clusters. Omit 85–95% of individual leaves, needles, fine twigs. Represent as grouped masses, broken lobes, or clipped shadow shapes. If the illustration reads as lace, filigree, or dense engraving at thumbnail size, merge forms and remove marks.

**Chromatic Structure — One Added Print Hue:**
Identify the photo's dominant hue family and temperature. Choose ONE added high-chroma print hue by relationship:
- Quiet harmony: more saturated analogous hue
- Focused counterpoint: complementary or near-complementary hue
- Temperature bridge: warm→cool or cool→warm transition hue
The added hue MUST share source-derived shapes with the illustration (e.g., the silhouette of a tree becomes the carrier of the hue). It must NOT float as decoration. Use the hue as chromatic structure that changes balance, movement, or figure–ground. Natural colors in the photo section do not count as extra hues. Use no more than two neutral ink values (graphite, charcoal) besides paper tone and the single added hue.

**Torn-Paper Edge (Photo–Illustration Transition):**
The boundary where photography meets paper MUST be a visible hand-torn fibrous edge — NOT a clean digital clip, NOT a crisp rectangular mask, NOT a sticker border. Build the torn edge with:
- Irregular hand-ripped contour with shallow notches, uneven rises, soft scallops, occasional longer fiber pulls
- Narrow feathered fringe (1–4% of poster's short edge) of exposed paper fibers and warm paper tone
- Slight local thinning, abrasion, or broken emulsion near selected segments
- Natural asymmetry: some segments nearly straight, some softly ragged, one or two stronger torn pressure points
- Flat scan behavior with no artificial lifted-paper depth or heavy drop shadows
The torn contour should be visibly legible along 35–70% of the photographic perimeter. Allow illustration or chromatic structure to cross or emerge from selected torn segments.

**Micro-Text System:**
Add ONE restrained micro-text element near a compositional pressure point (not as a title banner). Language: English only — no Chinese characters anywhere on the poster. Placement: bottom edge, side margin, or within quiet space. Typography: small serif or typewriter font, integrated into the paper surface (letterpress or rubber-stamp feel). Content: a short English phrase (2–5 words) derived from the scene's mood or subject. Do NOT weaken the image hierarchy with oversized text.

**Mood & Atmosphere:**
Calm, tactile, contemplative. Like a page torn from a well-loved art zine. Quiet confidence, handmade warmth, editorial restraint.

**Hard Avoids:**
No digital collage effects, no drop shadows on photo edges, no decorative frames, no gradient backgrounds, no clip-art illustrations, no busy patterns, no more than one added hue, no clean vector look, no commercial poster aesthetic, no AI-generated smooth plastic look, no stock photo overlay, no watermark, no border.

Vertical poster, 3:5 ratio, high resolution. The result should read clearly at thumbnail size and feel intentionally unfinished at normal viewing size.
